I always loved digital cameras butttttt. . . I really did not feel like buying a new one -- even with my big girl money. I was quite happy with my phone camera, I mean I had various filters at my arsenal -- what more could I wish for? I was using apps such as Dazzcam and even my phone's native camera filtering features, and I was very content with them. If anything, it made me like my phone even more because someone from 1723 would definitely faint looking at the witchcraft I am doing with this glowing, black box.

Of course, seeing everyone hop on the trend of purchasing a digicam did kind of sway my feelings since I, admittedly, am a trendhopper. However, taking one look at the prices and looking at what I could buy instead changed my mind. I would sigh sometimes whenever I would see my homegirls posting chic going out photos, because they looked so, so, so good. It wasn't even the pictures, it was just the experience of taking the photos. Like holding this little box that is barely the size of your palm -- it is so cute. . . so stylish. Whatever, a girl could dream.

I remember talking about this with my baba (dad) over the phone when we were Facetiming. I was telling him, jokingly, that everyone nowadays prefers the more "vintage" look to photos and have started purchasing digital cameras. He was confused. Very, very confused. He could not wrap his head around it, honestly. It was really funny, and to be honest, kind of valid? Oh well. Anyways. He then told me that he actually owns a digital camera. Although it is old, like 15-years-old, she still works fine because my baba is a very Type A man and meticulously takes care of his belongings. He showed the camera to me and I fell in LOVE. That's how my baba's little red Sanyo Xacti-CG88 became mine!

I think I found Sally Hansen’s Miracle Gel during a Walgreens visit, most likely during my first year into high school. Not too long before starting college, I had bare nails for most of my life since playing classical guitar, which meant using sand paper to shape and smoothen the nails on my right hand that picked the strings while the nails on my left hand were cut as decently short as possible in order to press the frets. The only exceptions were the long breaks or evening events, where I would paint my nails a vivid pearlescent color.

This past August, however, I found myself gravitating towards a dark, vivid shade of red labeled Wine Stock in the CVS on the corner block of Astor Pl just two days before the first day of classes. I was looking for a polish color that would visually mirror my mindset going into this school year to “follow my heart,” and envisioned the deep intensity of the hue to resemble the internal resources that I no longer have reason for laboring to give and offer; rather, these internal things are all learning to be kept still, processed, and grown even stronger for myself. I don’t think I’ll ever be able to play with falsies or extensions like Dolly Parton could, but painting my natural, shortened nails every two weeks with Wine Stock became a newfound ritual of self-expression and self-care; a semi-permanent wearable lucky charm.

I don’t have a specific lucky charm to call lucky, but if I had to choose one for the sake of this assignment it’d probably be a lion necklace I usually wear. I got it at some thrift store in Hong Kong for $10 USD (80HKD) in 2023 and never wore it since it looked kinda chunky and just this thing hanging around my neck. It’s a gold-plated necklace of a lion head with a thin chain. I think ever since I moved to New York for school it’s been the only necklace I wear. Not that it has much sentimental value or grants me luck, but I’ve grown to like the clunkiness of it and the connotations of a lion. But since I wear it almost everyday it’s starting to wear out, and the gold (/gold-looking) plating is coming off and you can see some cooper-y patches underneath. I try my best to take care of it (as in not getting it near water), but over time it’s inevitable it’ll start wearing out, which is kinda sad I guess. Hoping to somehow eternalize it. It doesn’t bring me luck, but wearing it makes me feel like I have a little lion guardian guarding me at all times — like those Chinese guardian lion statues at the front of buildings. If I were to be really superstitious, this little necklace wards off evil spirits for me. It has taken care of me well and only good things have happened since.

I am definitely a superstitious person, but the first thing that comes to mind when I think of a lucky charm is sort of the opposite. When my family visited Paris in 2024, I split off from them and explored different art museums every day. While potentially cliché, Vincent Van Gogh is one of my absolute favorite artists. Luckily for me, the Musée d’Orsay was hosting a Van Gogh exhibition focusing on the final months of his life. It was there I was able to see his final painting ever—the one he was working on the same day he died—“Tree Roots.” It was quite emotional for me. I stood in front of it until my feet hurt. I purchased a small magnet of it at the gift shop.

Since then I’ve stayed in two different dorm rooms, two different bedrooms at my parent’s houses, and just recently decorated my current apartment. The magnet has been taped up above my pillow in each of them. It’s also my phone wallpaper. I like seeing it repeatedly, even as the constant exposure makes me eventually stop noticing it each time. I find it an aesthetically beautiful painting, and the context only gives it more gravity.

It’s maybe a bit morbid for an object I center so significantly. If I sit and think about it for too long it’ll make me sad, but in the quick glances I afford it daily, I am only appreciating its beauty. I always have the option to reflect on it if I’d like, it’ll wait for me to get into the mindset to do so. I think that’s what draws me to it so much. The magnet has followed me through so many major changes, and its consistent location anchors me a bit. In that sense, I think it qualifies as a lucky charm or habit—seeing the painting, in whatever form it’s in, brings me a little comfort.

I’ve always been interested in analog techniques and devices in design, and in the past few years have heavily relied on my scanner to produce my art. I use it for both school and work, and because of its relatively immobile quality I have grown to be attached to working in my room where I have access to everything I need. My workspace has become so personal and tailored to my interests that I find it hard to produce work I truly love and can proudly say is a reflection of myself if I don’t have access to my scanner and printer.

To boil it down further, I basically have a small collection of devices (scanner, printer, magazines…) that I’ve become very attached to. I love the way they work with me and with each other and have accepted their faults as unique and even beneficial to my own work. So much so that I refuse to replace them until I absolutely have to - the thought of finding a new object to replace my old object (which I know and love) seems impossible to commit to if I don’t have a proper reason - in this case, that reason is absolute failure of its function. I’m a firm believer that every object has more value than it lets on, and discarding it before fully exploring it is a crime. I think it is such an important ritual to use your objects to its full potential, then mis-use them to their even fuller potential, and in turn be rewarded with experiences and results that are truly a product of yourself.

Funny how I came up with the idea “hey let’s all write something about lucky charms,” without carefully considering what my lucky charm might be. By “carefully considering,” I'm already suggesting that nothing immediately came to my mind, so I had to think carefully and hard. There was a time in elementary school when I firmly believed that certain pairs of socks could bring me luck, so I would wear them on exam days. I remember rescuing my beloved socks from the trash after my mom decided that they were too worn out. Till this day, I must have lost all the socks I owned back then. The things I own now that most closely resemble lucky charms are my jade stones. All my jade stones come from my mom’s collection. I believe these beautiful stones have spirits within them that can bless me and keep me safe. Maybe they also bring me luck. There is a particular jade buddha necklace that I “stole” from my aunt when I was two. She was showing off her jades to my mom, and when she was packing everything up, she noticed that the jade buddha–her new favourite--was missing. I have no memory of all of this, including how they eventually found it tucked under my clothes, near my stomach. A family friend who’s “the jade expert” heard the story and excitedly declared that I have special spiritual connection (缘分) with this particular stone, and my aunt (not completely willingly) gifted it to me. I try not to take off my jade necklace and bracelet unless I’m rock climbing. I’m also wearing a bracelet that another aunt bought me from the Jade Buddha Temple in Shanghai, wishing me safety and luck away from home on the other side of the ocean.

I’m more of an animist than a buddhist. Something else I believe can bring me luck is the bad (unlucky) things that happen to me. I believe fortune and misfortune work like a wicked seesaw–whenever something unfortunate happens to me, I will get a lucky ticket that will redeem itself at some point in the near future. On the other hand, whenever I feel particularly lucky or when I'm really enjoying my life and having a good time, beneath the bubbles of happiness and lightheartedness lurks the suspicion that something terrible is bound to happen soon.
